When Gamification Does Not Work?

The uncomfortable truth Bolt points onto a course that does not engage and they will not save it. Pedagogy first — gamification amplifies, it does not manufacture.
🎁 Reward inflation
Pull the points away — engagement collapses with them.
Fix: Reward progress, not attendance. Badges = recognition, not bribes.
⚖️ Public leaderboards humiliate
For adults returning after decades, public ranking discourages — it does not motivate.
Fix: Rank against past self. Personal progress, not class league.
🥦 "Chocolate-covered broccoli"
A boring task in cartoon wrapping is still boring. Adults notice — and trust you less.
Fix: Fix the task, not the wrapper. Would it work without the points?
⚙️ Tool overload & setup tax
Three apps, two accounts, one frustrated learner walking out.
Fix: One tool. One login. Browser-based. Test on the least confident learner.
🧓 Wrong audience, wrong tone
Cartoon visuals and "Level Up!" pop-ups feel patronising to voluntary adult learners.
Fix: Match the register. Grown-up gamification for grown-ups.
📊 No feedback loop
Pilot a tool. See no change. Conclude it "does not work" — without ever asking the learners.
Fix: Ask*. Adapt or kill. Do not hope.
🧭
THE TAKEAWAY
Most failed gamification projects fail quietly — because no-one asked the learners.
* The three questions to ask after each pilot:
1Did it help you learn?
2What would you change?
3Would you keep using it?
